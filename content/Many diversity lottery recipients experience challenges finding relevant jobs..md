Many diversity lottery recipients experience challenges finding relevant jobs. Hailu et al. ([[@2012hailu_lived#^ec4994|2012]]) found that participants in their study reported having difficulty finding jobs due to biased perceptions against their accent, [[Experiences that align with our habitus are more valued than others.|regardless of their credentials]]. This is congruent with the more general idea that [[marginalized people are rarely seen as experts.]] Additionally, [[many diversity lottery recipients have high levels of debt]] due to the cost of airfare and visa fees. They also often are expected to support not only their families in the U.S. but also back home, [[Marginalized students often have more responsibilities to their families and communities than others.|as is more generally the case with recent immigrants and marginalized people.]] In this position, they are forced to take whatever work they can find, often jobs that are less desirable, dangerous, or exploitative. For this reason, [[many diversity lottery recipients plan to return to their home country.]]
