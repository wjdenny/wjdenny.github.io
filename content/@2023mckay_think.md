## Notes
> Feynman was so successful in his career and hobbies because he always kept a running list of questions or problems in his head. These open questions created a “[serendipity engine](https://adjacentpossible.substack.com/p/the-serendipity-engine)” that allowed him to attract insights no matter what he was doing. Any time Feynman encountered novel information, he applied it to the puzzles he had simmering on the back burner of his mind. Now and then, he’d find a key that unlocked a professional or private breakthrough. #pkm

In this article, [[Richard Feynman]]'s technique of [[Twelve Favorite Problems]] seems to be used more as a way of promoting interdisciplinary thinking rather than triaging learning experiences.
