Learning and acquisition are distinct neurological processes.

%%
The distinction between cognitive learning and skill acquisition, particularly through muscle memory or kinesthetic learning, has been a topic of interest in educational psychology and neuroscience. While there is some merit to the idea that these two types of learning involve different processes, there is also significant overlap and interaction between cognitive and motor learning systems.

One of the key scholars in this field is Richard Schmidt, whose work has focused on motor learning and skill acquisition. His research has highlighted the importance of practice and feedback in developing motor skills, as well as the role of cognitive processes in skill acquisition. Schmidt's schema theory, proposed in the 1970s, suggests that skilled performance arises from the organization of movements into generalized motor programs, which can be adjusted based on feedback from the environment.

Another influential figure is Daniel Kahneman, whose work in cognitive psychology has shed light on the dual-process theory of thinking. Kahneman's research distinguishes between fast, intuitive thinking (System 1) and slow, deliberate thinking (System 2). While System 1 processes may be more closely associated with motor learning and muscle memory, System 2 processes play a role in planning, strategy, and conscious decision-making, which are crucial aspects of skill acquisition in domains like mathematics and history.

Recent advances in neuroscience have also provided insights into the neural mechanisms underlying cognitive and motor learning. Studies using techniques such as functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) have revealed overlapping but distinct brain regions involved in these two types of learning. For example, the cerebellum is critical for motor learning and coordination, while the prefrontal cortex is involved in executive functions such as planning and decision-making.

In terms of articles, Schmidt's work on motor learning, particularly his book "Motor Control and Learning: A Behavioral Emphasis," provides a comprehensive overview of the theoretical and practical aspects of skill acquisition. Kahneman's book "Thinking, Fast and Slow" offers insights into the cognitive processes underlying decision-making and problem-solving. Additionally, research articles in journals such as "Journal of Motor Behavior," "Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition," and "Neuroscience" provide empirical evidence and theoretical frameworks for understanding the differences and interactions between cognitive and motor learning.
