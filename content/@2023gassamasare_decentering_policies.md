## Notes
> In this era, when someone says or does something inflammatory, people flock to their social media pages to see who they are and where they work and urge their employer to fire them for their transgression. A company today would be irresponsible if it didn't have any language regarding social media conduct and expectations around usage. It's important to have clearly laid-out policies that employees are expected to adhere to, particularly policies that are racially equitable. [page 49]

[[Organizations should have clear policies to describe what employees cannot post on social media.]]

> The problem was that there *wasn't* this same clarity--or any specific policies at all-- around resolving issues when customers treated the employees poorly. This discrepancy was further complicated by racial dynamics on the team that centered whiteness. [page 50]

> a white female employee was harassed by a customer, and the company's response had been quite different: it took swift actions against the customer who had harassed the white employee. This same protection wasn't extended to the Black employee. [page 50]

> It's time we do away with the "customer is always right" mantra. It can actually prove harmful. As someone who spent a lot of my youth working in retail, I experienced mistreatment at the hands of customers who were abusing company policies because they knew that their status, clout, wealth, and whiteness allowed them to do so. [page 50--51]

> Power dynamics further subjugate racially marginalized employees. The "customer is always right" mantra unfairly impacts non-white employees. [page 51]

> There were a number of policies in place for when students experienced harm, but very few policies for employees who experienced racial harm. [page 52]

One thing I'm noticing as I read her re-telling of lived experiences of racism, my first instinct is often to minimize or generalize it. As in, that can happen to anyone; it wasn't because of racism. I'm picking at that thread to figure out why I keep doing that.

> One police officer even insinuated that I could have known the robber since he was Black and I was Black. [page 53]

> In 2018, Starbucks was in the headlines for the arrest of two Black men. A white manager had called the police on two Black men who were in a Philadelphia Starbucks for a meeting. The manager told these men that they couldn't be in the location if they didn't order anything and then called the police on them, resulting in their arrest. [page 54]
